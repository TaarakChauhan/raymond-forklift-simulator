It does not replace formal operator training, a practical evaluation by your employer, or certification under OSHA 29 CFR 1910.178(l) in the United States or Ontario Regulation 851 and CSA B335 in Ontario. Always follow your site rules and the operator manual for your truck. The trucks are generic models styled after Raymond trucks and are not official Raymond products.

    Touch and game controller

    On a phone or tablet, use the stick on the left to drive and steer and the buttons on the right for the forks, horn, brake and camera.

    With a game controller: triggers or the left stick drive, the left stick steers, the right stick lifts, the pad tilts and sideshifts, the bumpers reach, A is the horn, B brakes, Y changes camera, X is the belt, Back is the parking brake and Start opens the menu.

    Safe habits the simulator checks

    • Travel with the forks low, about 4 to 6 inches off the floor, and tilted back.
    • Slow down when turning. Fast turns, especially with a raised load, can tip the truck.
    • Never pick up more than the capacity shown in the panel. Capacity drops as the load gets higher or further out.
    • Sound the horn at the crosswalk and at aisle ends. Give way to people on foot.
    • Stay under the 5 mph site limit, wear your seat belt or tether, and look in the direction of travel.
    • Park with the forks on the floor and the parking brake set.

    Counterbalanced trucks steer from the back

    The sit down and stand up trucks steer with the rear wheels, so the back swings wide when you turn. Leave room on that side.

    Picking up a pallet

    Line up square, bring the forks to the height of the pallet openings, drive in until the pallet touches the backrest, lift a little, tilt back, then back out slowly.
